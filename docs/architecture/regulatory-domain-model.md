\# Regulatory Domain Model

\*\*Version:\*\* 1.0

\*\*Status:\*\* Draft

\*\*Series:\*\* AI Architecture

\## 1. Purpose

The Regulatory Domain converts published FoundryAI Knowledge into structured,

business-applicable regulatory requirements and executable pathways.

It does not replace the Knowledge layer.

Knowledge answers:

> What does the authoritative source say?

The Regulatory Domain answers:

> What does this mean for this type of business, and what must happen next?

\## 2. Boundary

Knowledge Layer

&#x20; ↓

Regulatory Requirement

&#x20; ↓

Applicability

&#x20; ↓

Pathway

&#x20; ↓

Pathway Step

&#x20; ↓

Agency / Government System

&#x20; ↓

Completion / Evidence

The Regulatory Domain MUST preserve a citation back to the underlying

published Knowledge.

\## 3. Core Concepts

\### Regulatory Requirement

A structured obligation, registration, licence, filing, approval, renewal,

or other regulatory action that may apply to a business.

A requirement MUST have:

\- stable identity

\- jurisdiction

\- title

\- description

\- regulatory domain

\- requirement type

\- authoritative knowledge reference

\- applicability rules

\- status

\### Applicability

Defines when a regulatory requirement applies.

Applicability may depend on:

\- business structure

\- industry

\- activity

\- employee count

\- location

\- ownership

\- turnover

\- other verified Business Knowledge

Applicability MUST be explicit and testable.

\### Pathway

An ordered sequence of regulatory steps required to achieve a business

objective.

Examples:

Company Incorporation

Business Licence

NIB Registration

A pathway is not itself legal authority. Its steps are derived from

published Knowledge and verified regulatory requirements.

\### Pathway Step

A concrete action within a pathway.

A step may:

\- require information

\- require a previous step

\- produce evidence

\- interact with an agency

\- interact with a government system

\- create inputs required by a later step

\### Agency

The authoritative organization responsible for a requirement or step.

\### Government System

A portal, online service, submission system, or other government-operated

system through which a step may be completed.

\## 4. Relationship Types

The Regulatory Domain SHALL support at minimum:

\- prerequisite

\- dependency

\- produces

\- requires

\- satisfies

\- supersedes

\- alternative

\- related

Example:

Company Incorporation

&#x20; ↓ produces

Certificate of Incorporation

Business Licence

&#x20; ↓ produces

Business Licence Number

Business Licence Number

&#x20; ↓ required by

NIB Registration

\## 5. Evidence

Every regulatory requirement MUST reference the published Knowledge evidence

from which it was derived.

The reference MUST identify:

\- knowledge\_pack\_id

\- knowledge\_source\_id

\- chunk\_id

\- section / clause when available

The Regulatory Domain MUST NOT copy authoritative text without retaining

provenance.

\## 6. Business Knowledge

Regulatory applicability consumes verified Business Knowledge.

It does not directly ask the founder for information that already exists in

the Business Knowledge model.

Example:

Business Knowledge:

&#x20; legal structure = limited company

Requirement:

&#x20; certificate of incorporation required

The applicability engine evaluates the requirement against the existing

Business Knowledge.

\## 7. Pathway Dependencies

Dependencies must be explicit.

Example:

Company Incorporation

&#x20; ↓

Business Licence

&#x20; ↓

NIB Registration

NIB Registration may require:

\- Business Licence Number

\- Certificate of Incorporation

Those inputs may be populated from verified outputs of earlier pathway steps.

\## 8. Agency and System Registry

Agencies and government systems are separate concepts.

An Agency represents the responsible institution.

A Government System represents a digital or operational system used to

complete a requirement.

A system MUST reference its owning agency where known.

\## 9. Completion Evidence

A completed pathway step SHOULD produce structured evidence.

Examples:

\- certificate

\- registration number

\- licence number

\- confirmation

\- receipt

\- submitted application

\- approved application

Evidence generated by a pathway step may become Business Knowledge after

verification.

\## 10. Lifecycle

Requirements and pathways MUST support controlled lifecycle states.

Proposed:

draft

validated

published

superseded

withdrawn

Published regulatory definitions MUST NOT be silently mutated.

Corrections create a new version.

\## 11. Safety Boundary

The Regulatory Domain MUST NOT:

\- invent legal requirements

\- assign legal authority independently of Knowledge

\- hide uncertainty

\- silently infer applicability from missing data

\- silently change a pathway because an external system changed

\- treat an agency website as legal authority merely because it is official

Where authoritative evidence is insufficient, the requirement remains

unresolved or requires human review.

\## 12. Initial Bahamas Pathway

Initial pathway:

Company Incorporation

&#x20; ↓

Business Licence

&#x20; ↓

NIB Registration

NIB Registration is explicitly downstream.

Its inputs may include outputs from earlier steps.

\## 13. Initial Government Systems

National Insurance Board

NIB Online Portal

NIB Registrant Self-Service

NIB Employer Self-Service

These systems belong in the official systems registry and are separate from

the regulatory requirements themselves.

\## 14. Design Principle

The Regulatory Domain is the bridge between:

authoritative knowledge

and

founder-facing execution.

It must remain deterministic wherever possible and must preserve the exact

evidence that supports every requirement.
